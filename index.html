Theia

People living with dementia, and people who are just forgetful, lose track of ordinary things. The glasses are on the desk. The earbud case is by the keyboard.

Theia is a tabletop turret. You ask for the object. It looks around the room, circles it on screen, and gives the angle a laser can point to.

  1. Speak You say what you are looking for, or type it. When you finish speaking, the search starts.
  2. Seven photos A phone camera takes seven photos.
  3. Mark Theia picks the best match, draws a mark on it,
  4. Angle and shows azimuth and elevation in degrees.

Who it is for

At home, when something is missing

People living with dementia, and people who are just forgetful, lose ordinary things in the room they already know. The glasses are on the desk. The earbud case is by the keyboard. Asking someone in a group chat does not put a point on the object.

Theia stays in that room. They ask out loud, the camera looks, and a mark plus an angle show where it is. They do not have to remember the drawer or learn another app.

In the room with a provider

A visit slows down when the glasses, keys, or medication are somewhere in the room and nobody can see them. A healthcare professional, or the person with them, can ask for that one object and get a mark and an angle instead of searching the desk.

The same question works in a clinic closet or at a shared desk. The right object, in the room, at the moment they need it.
